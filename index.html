Romans 1
4th Sunday of Advent [A, #10]
Ordinary Time, Week 28, Mon [I, #467]

1 Paul, a servant of Jesus Christ, called to be an apostle, separated unto the gospel of God, CCC

2 Which he had promised before, by his prophets, in the holy scriptures,

3 Concerning his Son, who was made to him of the seed of David, according to the flesh, CCC

4 Who was predestinated the Son of God in power, according to the spirit of sanctification, by the resurrection of our Lord Jesus Christ from the dead; CCC

5 By whom we have received grace and apostleship for obedience to the faith, in all nations, for his name; CCC

6 Among whom are you also the called of Jesus Christ:

7 To all that are at Rome, the beloved of God, called to be saints. Grace to you, and peace from God our Father, and from the Lord Jesus Christ.

8 First I give thanks to my God, through Jesus Christ, for you all, because your faith is spoken of in the whole world.

9 For God is my witness, whom I serve in my spirit in the gospel of his Son, that without ceasing I make a commemoration of you;

Built for Prayer and Study

Everything you need to read Scripture as the Church reads it — designed for daily use on any device.

Today's Readings

See the daily Mass readings at a glance, with links directly into their biblical context. Install on your home screen for one-tap access.

Liturgical Annotations

Color-coded margin bars show every time a passage appears in the Lectionary for Mass and the Liturgy of the Hours, so you can see how the Church weaves Scripture through the liturgical year.

Catechism References

Inline CCC references connect the biblical text directly to the Catechism of the Catholic Church. Tap a reference pill to read the paragraph in context.

Instant Navigation

Press / or Cmd+K on any page to jump instantly to any book or chapter. Arrow keys move between chapters.

Works Offline

As a Progressive Web App, today's readings are cached automatically. Add to your home screen for instant, offline access — even without a connection.

Dark Mode

A responsive dark mode follows your system preferences (on chapter pages), making it comfortable for evening reading.

About the Project

The Liturgy Bible visualizes the liturgical use of the biblical text. You can learn more by reading the original white paper here.

Annotations are compiled from:

  • USCCB: Liturgical Calendar for the Dioceses of the US
  • Felix Just, S.J.: The Catholic Lectionary Website
  • Liturgical Press: Liturgy and Life Study Bible
  • Matthew P Hazell: Index Lectionum
  • 2020 Notitiae: M. Lessi Ariosto, Commento al ciclo biennale…

The biblical text uses two public domain translations: the Douay-Rheims (DRA) and King James Version (KJV). We have requested permission for several modern liturgical translations.
